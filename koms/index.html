Home Assistant, installed — and connected to your AI

Two ways to get Home Assistant OS onto a PC, and one clear path to letting Claude, ChatGPT or a coding agent run your house through it. Written for people who have never done this before.

  • 1 · The official way Download the image, write it to a USB stick with Etcher, boot the PC. The route Home Assistant documents itself.
  • 1 · One stick, a few questions An installer ISO that fetches the current image for you and writes it to the disk you pick. Fourteen languages, nothing to type.
  • 2 · Give an AI access Turn on Home Assistant's Model Context Protocol server, so an AI agent can read your sensors and switch things on.
  • 3 · Connect the client Claude, ChatGPT, Claude Code, Codex, opencode — what to paste where, and what each one needs.

Which install route?

Both end in the same place: a computer running Home Assistant OS, reached at homeassistant.local:8123 from a browser on your network. The difference is how much you do by hand.

OfficialOne-stick installer
You prepare Image file, USB stick, BIOS settings ISO file, USB stick, BIOS settings
The disk gets written by Etcher, on your normal computer The stick itself, on the target machine
Getting the image You find and download it It downloads the current version during the install
Screen during install Etcher's progress bar A short wizard: language, network, disk
Needs internet Only to download the image Yes — unless you point it at an image you already have
Second computer needed Yes, to write the stick No, though it is easier to download the ISO on one

If you have a laptop and a spare PC, either works and the official route is fine. If the machine you are installing on is the only computer in the house, or you would rather answer four questions than find the right image file, take the installer.

Both routes erase a disk

Home Assistant OS takes the whole disk it is written to. There is no dual boot, and nothing on that disk survives. Move anything you care about first, and pick the disk carefully — this is the one step nobody can undo.

Then connect an AI

Home Assistant can act as a Model Context Protocol server. That is a standard way for an AI app to ask your house questions and give it orders: "is the garage door open", "turn the kitchen light off". You switch it on in the Home Assistant interface — there is nothing to install — and then paste one URL into whichever AI app you use.

What the AI can reach is not automatic. It can only touch entities you have exposed to voice assistants, so the exposure list is where you decide how much of your home the AI may see. That is covered step by step in MCP.

What you need before starting

  • A computer that will become the Home Assistant machine. A small mini-PC, an old desktop or a thin client is plenty.
  • 2 GB of RAM at the very least, 4 GB to be comfortable. 32 GB of storage.
  • A USB stick, 8 GB or more. It will be erased.
  • A wired network connection is strongly recommended for the first start. Wi-Fi works, but Ethernet removes a whole category of problems.
  • About half an hour, most of it waiting.

Start with the official install, or with the one-stick installer.
