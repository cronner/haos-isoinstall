Install Home Assistant on a PC

Home Assistant is a free program that runs your smart home — your lights, your heating, your sensors — from a computer in your own house. It keeps working when the internet doesn't, and your data stays with you.

This page shows you how to put it on an old PC. You don't need to know anything about Linux, and you won't type a single command. You make one USB stick, the stick asks you a few questions, and it does the rest.

The PC you use will be completely emptied. Everything on its disk is erased and cannot be brought back. Use a spare computer you don't need any more — never your everyday PC. If there is anything on it you want to keep, copy it to another disk first.

What you need

  • A spare PC. Any normal desktop, mini PC or laptop from the last ten years with an Intel or AMD processor. It will be wiped and used only for Home Assistant.
  • A USB stick you don't mind erasing, 2 GB or bigger.
  • An internet connection. A cable from the PC to your router is the easiest on the first day; you can set up Wi-Fi later from Home Assistant itself.
  • About half an hour. Most of it is waiting, not working.

This is for a normal PC. A Raspberry Pi or another small board uses a different file — see the official installation page for those.

The steps

  1. Download the installer

    It is a single file of about 450 MB. You don't have to choose a version: the stick fetches the newest Home Assistant for you when it installs.

    Get the installer

    On the Releases page, download the file ending in .iso. You can ignore the other file next to it.

  2. Put it on the USB stick

    Plug the stick into your normal computer and use a free program called Etcher. Open it, pick the file you just downloaded, pick the stick, and click Flash.

    Don't drag the file onto the stick like a photo. A stick prepared that way won't start the computer.

  3. Change two settings on the spare PC

    Restart the spare PC and tap F2 (or Del) as it starts up. A settings screen appears — the one the manufacturer calls BIOS or Setup.

    Find Secure Boot and turn it off. Then find SATA mode (it may be called Storage or SATA configuration) and set it to AHCI. Save and exit.

    Don't worry about the other settings. These two are the only ones that matter, and you can't break anything by looking.

  4. Start the PC from the stick

    Leave the stick plugged in and turn the PC on. If it starts its old system instead, restart it and tap F12 or F10 while it boots, then choose the USB stick from the list.

  5. Answer the questions

    The stick starts a small menu. Pick your language — the right keyboard is chosen for you. Then it asks how the PC is connected to the internet, and finally which disk to install on.

    Nothing is written to a disk until you confirm. Read the disk list carefully: that is the disk that gets erased.

  6. Wait, then take the stick out

    The installer downloads Home Assistant, checks the download and writes it to the disk. It shows you what it is doing. This takes between five and twenty minutes, depending on your internet.

    When it says it is finished, remove the USB stick and let the PC restart.

The languages you can pick

The menu and its messages come in the fourteen languages used in the countries with the most Home Assistant installations. When you choose one, the keyboard is set up for you at the same time:

  • English
  • Danish
  • Czech
  • Dutch
  • Finnish
  • French
  • German
  • Italian
  • Norwegian
  • Polish
  • Portuguese
  • Russian
  • Spanish
  • Swedish

Using a keyboard from another country — a UK one, for example? The last line of the language menu sets the keyboard on its own, without changing the language.

First start of Home Assistant

  1. Connect the PC to your router with a cable and turn it on.
  2. On your phone or your normal computer — connected to the same network — open http://homeassistant.local in a browser.
  3. You will see Preparing Home Assistant. This can take twenty minutes or more. It has not frozen; leave it alone and come back.
  4. When the welcome screen appears, choose Create my smart home and fill in a name, a username and a password. Write the password down somewhere safe: there is no way to recover it.
  5. Set where your home is, finish, and you are in. From there you add your devices under Settings.

If it doesn't work

The PC starts its old system instead of the stick
Restart and tap F12 or F10 while it boots, then pick the USB stick. If the stick isn't in the list, flash it again with Etcher — the file must be written by a program, not copied.
The screen stays black after the PC starts
Go back into the settings screen (step 3) and check that Secure Boot is off. Leave the stick plugged in and try again.
The installer says it cannot find a disk
The PC's storage is probably set to RAID instead of AHCI. Change that setting, then start the stick again.
The address doesn't open in my browser
Some routers block that name. Try http://homeassistant.local:8123 first, and if that also fails, find the PC's address in your router's list of connected devices and open http://that-address:8123.
It has said "Preparing Home Assistant" for a long time
That is normal on a slow connection or an old disk. It is downloading Home Assistant. Give it half an hour.
Something about Home Assistant itself is wrong
Ask the Home Assistant community. This installer only puts the program on the disk.
